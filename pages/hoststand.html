Hoststand · The host stand, texting and reviews

Every guest conversation, from Google to the table to the review.

Guests find you on Google, join the waitlist or book, and get every update by text. After the meal, a two-question survey goes out with your review link, and low scores reach a manager while there's still time to fix it. Your host runs all of it from one iPad.

The gap

The host stand runs on four tools and a notepad.

The waitlist lives on paper

Quotes are guesses, guests crowd the door to hear their name, and nobody knows how many parties gave up and walked away.

Guests can't reach you

The phone rings mid-rush. Booking apps send texts one way. A guest running late has no one to tell, so the table sits empty.

Reviews arrive with no context

A one-star review shows up days later. The manager can't tell which visit it was, and the reply waits, or never happens.

How it works

One loop: get found, seat them, follow up, prove it.

Texting that runs the waitlist

Guests join from your Google listing, a QR code at the door, or the host. They get their place in line, a table-ready text, and a way to say they're running late. No app to download.

After the visit, a two-question survey goes out with your review link. Every guest gets the same link. When someone scores the night a 1, 2 or 3, a manager hears about it within minutes.

Reservations when you want them

Walk-in-only places can leave Book mode off and run the waitlist and inbox alone. Turn it on for a reservation book, reminders, no-show tracking and card holds for large parties.

The floor plan shows what's seated, reserved, bussing and open, and every booking is tied to the guest's conversation, so "running 15 late" lands where the host already is.

Every Google review, matched to the visit

Connect your Google Business Profile and new reviews land in the same inbox, matched to the visit when the guest is in your book. The manager sees when they were seated, who served them and what the survey said.

A suggested reply is drafted for each review. Nothing posts to Google until a person approves it.

A shift report the owner will actually read

Covers, actual wait against quoted wait, no-shows, parties that walked away, survey scores and the Google rating, for every shift.

Because the waitlist, the texts and the reviews live in one place, you can see what moved the rating, not just that it moved.

Under the hood

Built for a busy door, not a demo.

One thread per guest

Texts, Google messages, survey replies and reviews in one conversation, with every host's replies attributed.

Join from Google

Waitlist and booking links on your Google Business Profile. Guests never need an app or an account.

One-tap actions

Table ready, need more time, move to bar, seat at table 12. The common replies are buttons, not typing.

Guest memory

Visits, usual party size, allergies and seating preferences appear the moment a regular texts.

Surveys, done fairly

Every guest gets the same review invitation. Low scores also alert a manager, so problems get fixed instead of hidden.

Replies you approve

Suggested replies to Google reviews in your voice. A person approves every one before it posts.

Texting that stays compliant

Guest opt-in, STOP handling, quiet hours and carrier registration built in from the first message.

Your guests stay yours

Export your guest list, history and reviews whenever you want. No per-cover fees on your own regulars.

How it's built

The hard parts live in the software, not on the owner's to-do list.

Compliance built into the rules

Review requests follow the FTC's and Google's rules, and the flow editor won't publish a setup that breaks them. The safe way is the only way it runs.

Customer data stays with the business

Sales data from the point of sale is analyzed on the restaurant's own tablet. It isn't stored on Hoststand's servers.

Made for a dead zone

Wi-Fi drops at the host stand. Hoststand keeps working offline and catches up when the connection comes back, so the door never stops.

Pennies per restaurant to run

The hosting costs pennies per location, so the price can stay sized for one independent restaurant instead of a group.

Quiet problems, caught early

Patterns in each night's service point to the table that left unhappy without saying a word, while there's still time to make it right.

Who it's for

Independent restaurants and coffee shops with a busy door.

Places that don't want to pay per cover to seat their own regulars, and don't want four separate tools for the waitlist, texting, reviews and surveys.

  • Big tap targets for an iPad on a crowded host stand
  • Works for walk-in-only places, with reservations optional
  • Several hosts on one inbox, and a manager view for the GM
  • Priced for a single location, not a restaurant group
