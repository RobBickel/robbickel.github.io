FAAS · Actuarial modelling and mortality resilience

Will the premium last? Find out before the policy lapses.

An illustration assumes the same return every year. Markets don't deliver that, and the mortality table isn't your block either. FAAS runs every policy through thousands of market paths and mortality stresses. It shows the odds that coverage is still in force at the age that matters, and solves the premium that gets it there.

The same engine prices term business at a confidence level, studies your own deaths and lapses, projects cash flows and reserves under US GAAP LDTI, and steps assets and liabilities together, all from formulas you can read.

The gap

An illustration is one path. Your policyholders live on all of them.

Constant returns hide the risk

An 8% illustration and an average 8% are different policies. One bad sequence early on can drain the account value years before the illustrated lapse age.

The table isn't your block

Pricing tables carry loadings, and moving from the 2001 to the 2017 CSO table shifts charges by several percent. Then the insured lives arrive with their own health histories.

Spreadsheets run one policy at a time

A workbook macro can price one case. It can't test 1,500 policies across five scenarios and a thousand trials, then hand an auditor a record of exactly what went in.

What you see

Answers by product code, scenario and confidence level.

Probability in force, under every scenario

Watch how much of the block stays in force, year by year, under a base market, a fresh bootstrap, elevated mortality, longevity and a pandemic-with-crash scenario. Each line is weighted by face amount, so the policies that matter most count most.

Set testing points as a policy year or an attained age, such as life expectancy or age 100, and FAAS reports the probability of reaching each one.

Solvency bands, not a single line

For every product code, FAAS charts the policy fund (account values times survivors) as percentile bands across all trials. The median tells you what's likely. The 5th percentile tells you what you need to survive.

Mortality margin, from the insurer's side

Cost-of-insurance charges are priced on one basis; claims arrive on another. FAAS tracks the cumulative difference by product code, so you can see which block is quietly losing money, and by how much, under uncertainty.

In this illustrative block, one product code runs a median shortfall of about $57 million while another stays positive. A single average would have hidden both.

Your block, the way you already store it

Open model point files in the RPT and CSV formats actuarial teams already use. FAAS validates them and summarizes each product code, and writes them back without losing a field.

Each policy can carry its own comorbidity factor, with a default per product code for everything else.

Under the hood

Built for the questions actuaries get asked next.

Reproducible Monte Carlo

A counter-based random number generator means the same seed gives the same answer, whatever the number of cores.

Block-scale speed

7.5 million policy-trials in about a second on a 10-core laptop, using every core.

Premium solver

Finds the premium that keeps each policy in force to each testing point at your chosen confidence, such as 90%.

Comorbidity uncertainty

Experience mortality can differ from the pricing basis by an uncertain factor, drawn per trial, so the bands reflect what you don't know.

Mortality stresses

Level shocks, age-band shocks, catastrophe years and improvement, applied to charges, experience or both.

Results where you need them

Every run writes Parquet tables and can load PostgreSQL or SQL Server in one transaction. Re-running replaces the run instead of duplicating it.

An audit trail by default

Each run records its inputs with SHA-256 fingerprints, plus the seeds, version and who ran it.

No secrets in project files

Database passwords come from the environment or integrated sign-on. A connection string with a password in it is refused.

New run types · coming in the next download

Pricing, experience, reserving and projections in one project.

Term pricing at confidence

The level premium for 10 or 20 years at 95%, 90%, 80% or 70% confidence, across lapse rates, discount rates and mortality multipliers, with stochastic discount paths if you want them.

Experience studies

Actual-to-expected deaths and lapses by sex, age band and policy year from your own data, with fractional ages (UDD or Balducci). The calibrated basis feeds straight into pricing.

Claims projection

How much to hold for death claims over a period at your confidence level, with multiplier uncertainty, per-life reinsurance retention and premiums offset.

Projections

Deterministic or stochastic, annual or monthly: premiums, expenses, reserves, investment income and profit by product, grouped and rolled up the way you report.

US GAAP LDTI

Liability for future policy benefits by cohort, with net premium ratios, cumulative catch-up at the locked-in rate, the disclosure rollforward, AOCI at the current rate and DAC. IFRS 17 is an option.

Nested stochastic

Risk-neutral valuations at points along each real-world path, branching from that path's own state, with the values fed back into the outer projection.

Asset-liability strategy

Liabilities and a bond-and-cash fund stepped together each year: income, maturities, reinvestment, forced sales and realized losses, credited rates and the dynamic lapses they drive.

Formulas you can see

Every variable is a readable formula. Diagram View traces what feeds it and what it feeds, with live values for any policy, and every setting has a form in Studio.

Checked, not assumed

Tested against the actuary's own workbook.

FAAS's engine is tested against a working actuarial workbook and reproduces its results exactly, including the solved premiums. Actuarial input from Gary Flotron (UMSL) shaped what it measures and why.

  • Acceptance tests built from the original workbook's inputs and outputs
  • Every run's results folder can be traced back to the exact inputs
  • Same project, same seed, same numbers
  • Runs on your machines; your policy data never leaves them
