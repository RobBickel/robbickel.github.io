Companies without an in-house technology leader often reach a point where they know they need *someone*. Two options come up frequently: hiring a **fractional CTO**, or engaging a **managed software services** provider. They solve different problems, and choosing the wrong one is an expensive way to stay stuck.

## What a fractional CTO does

A fractional CTO is a part-time technology executive. Typical responsibilities:

- Technical strategy aligned with business goals
- Build-versus-buy decisions
- Vendor evaluation and negotiation
- Hiring plans and team structure
- Translating technical reality for leadership and the board
- Due diligence for acquisitions or investments

The key word is *leadership*. A fractional CTO decides and advises. They usually don't patch servers, fix bugs, or answer the 2 a.m. alert.

## What managed software services do

A managed software services provider takes hands-on, ongoing ownership of your applications:

- Monitoring and incident response
- Security patching and dependency updates
- Backups and tested restores
- Documentation and runbooks
- Bug fixes, changes, and enhancements
- Access and credential management
- Regular portfolio reviews

The key word is *operations*. The work is keeping the software you rely on healthy and improving, every week.

## How to tell which you need

| If your biggest problem is... | You probably need |
|---|---|
| "We don't know what technology direction to take" | Fractional CTO |
| "We're about to make a major platform or vendor decision" | Fractional CTO |
| "We need to hire a technical team and don't know how" | Fractional CTO |
| "Our critical app has no one maintaining it" | Managed software services |
| "We can't answer security questions about our systems" | Managed software services |
| "Small changes take months because nobody owns the code" | Managed software services |
| "We have too many tools and don't know what to cut" | Either, starting with an audit |

## When you need both

Many mid-sized companies need some of each: a few hours a month of strategic guidance, plus reliable hands-on ownership of the systems they already run. Those two roles work best when they're coordinated, because strategy that ignores operational reality fails, and operations without direction drift.
